A. The stagewise backend and the default choice (C-245, C-247)
--------------------------------------------------------------
The backend held: 320 random problems against the dense backend (no equalities, no inequalities,
one-sided, two-sided and coupling rows, variables in no row, one stage, an arrow, two independent
parts, stages of different sizes, random variable orders, coupled stage costs, infeasible and
unbounded problems, indefinite Hessians), statuses, x, y and z within 1e-6, iterations the same but
on two indefinite problems (one apart, where PIQP's own two backends are one apart too). 1 500
random patterns gave valid partitions; 3 000 multistage problems in random orders gave their stages;
stage_work's counts are what the kernels build; the block Cholesky to a condition of 4e9.

1. The default took the stagewise backend where it is slower than the sparse one it replaced:
   multistage problems with inequality rows over each stage and blocks of 5 or 6 slots, which the
   fit (24 problems with none) had not seen. Stagewise / sparse, measured and the model's:
     mpcpath_4_2_20_4    1.17-1.23   0.98
     mpcpath_4_2_10_4c   1.28        0.98
     mpcpath_4_2_60_6c   1.22        0.94     (eleven problems, all 1.17-1.33)
   After: the weights refitted to 57 problems, 23 with path rows and 10 Hessians of coupled dense
   blocks (results/stagewise_fit.md): the eleven take the sparse backend, the model at 1.26-1.38.
   Leaving each problem out of its own fit, the model picks the fastest on 50 of the 57, its worst
   pick 1.12x; the 55 problems of the IPM speed study and the race cars' SQP take what they took.
2. The embedded-QP case study's scaly_sparse became the stagewise backend from a horizon of 32
   (the model's margin 55.7 against 55.8 us); its README's times, sizes and Cortex-M7 tables are
   the sparse backend's. After: run_scaly.py, embedded_size.py and the notebook ask for sparse=True.
3. stages() ran a Python min over every offset for every unconnected part, and a variable nothing
   couples is a part: mpc_qp(4, 2, 400) with 5 000 of them, 16.4 s against 0.046 s for the choice
   before C-247. Each of them also grew every block when the blocks were full (3 of them: blocks of
   6 to 7, 1.36x the work). After: such variables are left out of the search, fill the padding,
   then take blocks of their own or a slot more in each block, whichever is less work: 0.09 s,
   blocks of 6. With the extra start from the first coupled variable the partition is no worse
   than before on any of 295 problems with such variables, and less work on 184.
4. A row that reads most variables (a budget row) made the condensed pattern dense, and the
   partition was found for nothing (two blocks): n = 4 000, 1.06 s and 1 074 MB more; n = 8 000,
   4.29 s and 4 061 MB. After: stagewise_floor_us, a lower bound from the row lengths, is past the
   other backends' time and the partition is not looked for: 0.99 s and 547 MB, 4.20 s and

B. The stage step's kernels (C-246)
-----------------------------------
Held: the default's C byte-identical before and after on 209 Functions and six targets; the JIT
key tells a running node from a pairwise one; 468 derivative checks; the running Cholesky on every
order 1 to 130 on 30 targets (22 custom tiles); the running solves, in place, hoisted, batched,
float32 and under Rosetta; no out-of-bounds access under the address sanitizer; the symmetric
product in every context tried (scan, vmap, callee, shared transpose, slices, panels, hints).

1. The symmetric product is not the full product to the last bit. At 19 rows on a target with
   vectors of two (armv8-a, x86-64) the full product computes entry (15, 18) in the scalar tile of
   the last column, which clang vectorizes with unfused multiplies and adds, and (18, 15) in a
   vector tile with fused ones: the full product is not symmetric, and the copy differs from it in
   one entry by 6.7e-16. The lower triangle is the full product's to the bit. After: the claim and
   the test say so, and the test has the case.
2. solve_triangular(..., sums="running") with one right-hand side is the pairwise C, and with two
   to a tile's width only its reciprocals change; the block solves' two passes are vector solves.
   The docs said otherwise. After: corrected.
3. cholesky's docstring said "about twice as fast"; the running form takes 0.62-0.85 of the
   pairwise one's time from 24 to 200 rows. After: corrected.
4. A backward substitution in the running form (lower with trans, or upper without), blocked, was
   slower than the pairwise one, which is blocked too from four tile widths:
     lower, trans   32x16 1.12-1.16   64x16 1.11-1.13   64x33 1.08-1.16
     upper          32x16 1.00        64x33 1.04
   After: from four tile widths it is the pairwise one (1.00 by construction); below, where the
   pairwise form is not blocked, it stays running (0.33-0.70 of the pairwise time).
5. The symmetric product was taken for reductions of four terms, where the copy across the
   diagonal costs more than the tiles skipped: symmetric / full, a @ a.T with a m x k,
     k = 4    28 rows 1.46   64 rows 1.27
     k = 8    28 rows 1.17
     k = 12   28 rows 1.04
     k = 16   28 rows 0.98   64 rows 0.79
   After: taken from sixteen terms. No corpus kernel and no stage step of a block with 16 or more
   coupling slots changes; a stage step with fewer now takes the full product.
