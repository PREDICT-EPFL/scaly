Case study E3 · Embedded convex MPC QPs (QOCO / QOCOGEN)

Plan case_studies_plan_2026_09_27.html, E3 · branch claude/case-studies · 27 Sep 2026 · code in examples/case_studies/embedded_qp/

Summary. QOCOGEN (Chari and Açıkmeşe, 2026) is the closest prior work to Scaly's generated PIQP: a generated interior-point solver specialized to one KKT pattern. Its LDLᵀ microbenchmark and its oscillating-masses MPC family were run with every solver, past the horizon where QOCOGEN stops, and compiled for a Cortex-M7.

  • The factorization.
    • Scaly's straight-line L D L' beats QOCOGEN's at T = 5 and 15.
    • Its looped one matches QOCOGEN from T = 50, with 2 kB of machine code and a 0.1 s build. QOCOGEN's whole solver needs 1.2 MB and 176 s.
    • Both Scaly schedules beat QDLDL throughout: the loops by 1.9 to 2.1×, straight-line code by up to 5.9×.
  • The whole solver: Scaly's generated PIQP is the fastest of seven at every horizon, 8 to 200.
    • 1.4 to 1.8× QOCOGEN.
    • 1.2 to 1.3× the PIQP library it is generated from, with the same iterations.
    • 1.4 to 1.7× QOCO and 3.7 to 4.9× Clarabel.
    • Only OSQP at its loose 1e-3 tolerance comes close, with objectives off by up to 1e-3.
  • Generation scales for Scaly and not for QOCOGEN.
    • QOCOGEN's generate plus compile grows from 15 s at T = 8 to 453 s at T = 56 (2 MB of machine code), and passes 40 minutes at T = 76, where the grid stops it.
    • Scaly builds the T = 200 solver in 5.4 s, with 54 kB of machine code, about what it needs at T = 8.
  • Embedded (Cortex-M7, sizes only). Scaly's flash is a fifth to a third of QOCOGEN's. Its RAM is 2.4 to 7.7× QOCOGEN's and grows quadratically, because it builds the Hessian dense from the parameters (CS-12). At T = 8 QOCOGEN fits an STM32F4 and Scaly doesn't.

Against the plan's success criteria: "faster than QOCOGEN at every horizon it can generate, and a solver at horizons it cannot" is met. The embedded leg is half met: sizes are measured but there is no board, and Scaly's RAM needs CS-12 fixed before it fits a small MCU. The CVXPYgen quadcopter was not reproducible (section 1).

1. The benchmarks and what changed from the plan

  • Upstream.
    • qoco-benchmarks at d7e00f5: the oscillating-masses family and its QOCO/QOCOGEN conversion and C timing harness.
    • qoco at 0198625, for its vendored QDLDL.
    • From PyPI: QOCO 0.3.2, QOCOGEN 0.1.9, Clarabel 0.11.1, OSQP 1.1.3, CVXPY 1.9.3.
    • QOCOGEN is compiled with the flags Scaly's JIT uses, -O2 -mcpu=native -fno-math-errno.
  • The CVXPYgen quadcopter is not reproducible. Its script and its data (A, B, weights, limits) were never published, and the paper reports times only in a figure. The oscillating masses, the QOCO paper's QP family, are public and fully specified, so they replace it.
  • CVXPYgen itself is left out. Importing it imports pdaqp, whose juliacall downloads Julia and writes ~/.julia on first import. OSQP and Clarabel are timed as libraries.
  • The LDLᵀ microbenchmark is rebuilt from the paper's description. QOCO's Appendix B script is not public. The LQR KKT matrix uses random A, B of the stated sizes and an unstated eps, taken here as 1e-7. QDLDL reproduces the paper's times to within 10% at every horizon, which suggests the matrix is right.
  • CS-6 (QP build time) did not need fixing first. The generated PIQP builds in under 6 s at every horizon up to 200.

4. What the numbers say about Scaly

  • Loops beat unrolling once the problem stops fitting in cache, and they keep generation cheap. QOCOGEN writes the factorization out entry by entry. That wins at T = 5 but costs minutes of generation, megabytes of code and, from T = 50, speed. Scaly's schedule choice (sparse_unroll) picks straight-line code only where it pays. The same choice makes the whole solver the fastest here at every size.
  • The generated PIQP beats the library it is generated from, by 1.2 to 1.3×, with identical iterations. That is the gain from specializing to one KKT pattern and removing the library's dispatch, the Tier 4 claim.
  • The embedded blocker is CS-12, and it is a clear fix. With Q, R as parameters, the generated solver zero-fills a dense n × n array every solve to build P, then reads its nonzeros back: about 1.6 n² doubles of workspace in all.
    • Without it the workspace would be an estimated 95 kB at T = 8, level with QOCOGEN's.
    • Scaly would then fit an STM32F4 wherever QOCOGEN does, and at T = 20, where QOCOGEN's flash no longer fits.
  • Scaly's C source grows with the horizon, 0.3 to 5.2 MB, because the KKT pattern's index tables are written as data. The machine code does not grow; the tables go into flash as .rodata (132 to 924 kB here).

5. Tests and checks

  • Existing tests. The paths this study exercises already had tests:
    • SparseLDL with a given ordering and in both schedules: tests/linalg/test_sparse_ldl.py, test_symbolic.py;
    • the generated PIQP against the library: tests/integration/test_qp_solvers_example.py.
  • New test, tests/integration/test_case_study_embedded_qp.py, for what they did not cover: the generated PIQP on a QP whose Hessian weights are parameters (the path behind CS-12). It runs against the PIQP library, iteration for iteration, on two weightings.
  • Mutation checks: 2 of 2 killed: perturbed q weights, and permuted r weights, reaching the generated solver.
  • Study gates. Every instance's objective is checked against Clarabel's, and the generated solver's iterations against the library's; section 3.2 records both.
  • Suite. The node-ID baseline is regenerated (+1). Nothing under src/ changed.

6. Fairness notes and what was not done

  • Machine. Not the reference machine. The grid ran over several hours on a shared workstation. Every timed cell waited for a one-minute load under 6; the other studies' compiles ran on other cores meanwhile.
  • Same compiler and flags for every generated solver. QOCOGEN is not built at its own -O3, so its T ≥ 50 numbers may understate it relative to the paper.
  • QOCOGEN's machine code is the whole solver, while Scaly's LDL-only numbers in 3.1 are the factorization alone. The whole-solver comparison is 3.2 and 3.3.
  • Not done.
    • A board: no cycle counts, only sizes.
    • CVXPYgen and its quadcopter (section 1).
    • The second-order-cone problems of QOCO's paper: Scaly's IPM is QP-only (CS-8).
