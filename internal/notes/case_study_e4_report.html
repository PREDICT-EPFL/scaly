Case study E4 · ALTRO

Plan case_studies_plan_2026_09_27.html, E4 · branch claude/case-studies · 27 Sep 2026 · code in examples/case_studies/altro/

Summary. ALTRO (IROS 2019) on its current implementation, Altro.jl 0.5, against the same algorithm generated by Scaly as one C function, on the paper's parallel park and cartpole.

  • Same algorithm, same iterates. Scaly's solver follows Altro.jl's source rather than the paper, in both phases: the augmented-Lagrangian iLQR, and the projected Newton polish.
    • It takes Altro.jl's iteration counts on every run: 18, 18, 112 and 93.
    • It reaches the same violation to every printed digit.
    • It returns the same trajectory to 1e-10.
  • 4.1 to 8.6× faster per cold solve.
    • Parallel park: 0.115 against 0.989 ms (augmented Lagrangian only), 0.128 against 1.009 ms (full ALTRO).
    • Cartpole: 3.51 against 14.5 ms, and 2.27 against 10.9 ms.
  • 13 to 33× faster to the first solution. 0.6 to 1.5 s from a fresh process, against 19 to 20 s for Julia's package load, setup and compile.
  • A finding about Altro.jl. Its projection restores feasibility but not optimality. Full ALTRO ends 2.4e-5 (parallel park) and 1.4e-4 (cartpole) above the augmented Lagrangian's objective, because Altro.jl 0.5's multiplier projection is commented out. Scaly reproduces this on purpose.
  • Nothing in src/ changed. The solver uses while_loop, scan, AD, sparse_jacobian through vmap, and the generated sparse L D L' with solve_with.

Against the plan's success criteria: "the same local optimum (cost within 1e-6 relative) in less wall time than current Altro.jl on every problem" is met on the two problems done. The objectives agree to 1e-10 relative. The other two problems (escape, quadrotor maze), the optional Kuka arm and the DIRCOL/IPOPT column are not done (CS-13).

1. The benchmark and the reproduction

  • Upstream. The paper's 2019 code lived in TrajectoryOptimization.jl.
    • The problem definitions are taken from problems/parallel_park.jl and problems/cartpole.jl at 320dbaca (2019-07-18).
    • They are ported to Altro.jl 0.5.0 (with TrajectoryOptimization 0.7.1, RobotDynamics 0.4.8 and RobotZoo 0.3.1) on Julia 1.10.12.
    • Same models (RobotZoo's DubinsCar and Cartpole(1.0, 0.2, 0.5, 9.81)), the same RK3, horizons (51 and 101 knots), bounds, goal constraints and initial controls.
    • One adaptation: the 2019 cost multiplied every stage term by dt and the current one does not, so Q and R are scaled by dt.
  • Two Altro.jl configurations. projected_newton=false is the augmented Lagrangian alone, to 1e-6. true is full ALTRO: the augmented Lagrangian to 1e-4, then the projection to 1e-6. Everything else is Altro.jl's defaults.
  • Two artifacts in a naive benchmark of Altro.jl, fixed in run_altro.jl.
    • benchmark_solve! restarts from the trajectory it finds, which after the first solve is the solution: a warm start. Here every sample restarts from the initial trajectory, and a check asserts that each timed solve took the recorded iteration count.
    • max_violation(::ALTROSolver) reads the projection's trajectory, which is never updated when the projection is off. The augmented-Lagrangian solver's own violation is reported instead.
  • Paper numbers are context only. Table I's 0.117 s for the parallel park (i7-6500U, the "preliminary implementation") is a hundred times current Altro.jl on this machine.

2. What was built

PieceWhat it does
al_ilqr.pyALTRO as one Scaly Function of the initial state, following Altro.jl 0.5's source (al_solve.jl, ilqr_solve.jl, forwardpass.jl, pn_solve.jl).
  • Outer while_loop (indexed): the multiplier and penalty updates, with Altro's tighter gradient tolerance on the last outer iteration.
  • iLQR while_loop: a rollout scan, a backward Riccati scan with negative strides, and a line search while_loop.
    • The backward pass builds its local models with sc.jacobian, sc.gradient and sc.hessian of RK3 and of the augmented stage cost, and factors Q_uu with linalg.cholesky.
    • The line search takes a ratio in [1e-8, 10] and at most 20 halvings.
    • Regularization follows Altro: ×1.6 growth, a 1e-8 floor, and +10 after a failed search.
    • A failed factorization skips the line search and raises the regularization. Altro.jl instead retries the backward pass inside the same iteration, which would count iterations differently; the equal counts show neither problem reaches that path.
  • Projected Newton: at most three linearizations, each factoring [H, Da'; Da, −δ] once and reusing it (SparseLDL.solve_with, the factor passed into a while_loop) while the violation's convergence rate stays above 1.1.
    • D is sc.sparse_jacobian of the vmapped stage constraints.
    • An inactive row is zeroed and given a −1 pivot, so one pattern covers every active set.
scaly_impl.pyThe two problems as OCPs: the model, diagonal weights, one inequality function per stage and a mask of the rows that apply. The first knot of the parallel park bounds the controls only.
run_scaly.py, compare.pyBuild, generate and compile with Scaly's JIT flags, then time from C with examples/qp_solvers/time_entry.c (200 cold solves; the C outputs are checked against the Python call's).
  • compare.py runs the four combinations × two problems in three fresh processes each, behind the quiet-machine gate, and keeps the fastest.
  • It recomputes each side's objective from its own trajectory.
  • --report rebuilds the table from the JSON.
baseline/setup.sh, julia_env/, run_altro.jlJulia 1.10.12 and a private depot in baseline/third_party, so nothing reaches ~/.julia, and a Manifest pinning the whole environment. run_altro.jl times the package load, the setup and the compiling first solve separately.
altro.ipynbThe study as a notebook. It runs both Scaly solvers on both problems without Julia, shows the projection's effect and the generated C's structure, and plots the recorded comparison and trajectories. RUN_BASELINES reruns compare.py.

4. What the numbers say

  • The L3 lever holds: a whole nested solver in one C function.
    • Three loops deep for the augmented Lagrangian: outer update, iLQR, line search.
    • Three more for the projection: linearization, reused-factor refinement, backtracking.
    • Every derivative comes from AD, and each backward-pass step is straight-line code.
    Against an allocation-free, StaticArrays-specialized Julia implementation this is 4 to 9× per solve. That is the "a few ×, not orders of magnitude" the plan predicted.
  • Where the speed-up shrinks. The cartpole's line search takes 3.5 rollouts per iLQR iteration, against 1.4 on the parallel park: 391 against 25 in all, counted with a scratch variant of al_ilqr.py that returns the line-search trial count.
    • A rollout is only the dynamics, and the derivative expansions are where Scaly's generated code differs most from ForwardDiff on StaticArrays.
    • So a rollout-heavy problem gains less. This is the likely reading: the split between the phases was not measured on either side.
  • Getting identical iterates took reading the source, not the paper. The details that decide the iteration count:
    • the line-search acceptance ratio and its upper bound;
    • the regularization schedule;
    • Altro's "gradient" (the mean over knots of maxi |di|/(|ui|+1));
    • the inner tolerance switching on the last outer iteration;
    • the outer stopping test on violation alone.
    Two further details matter here: the projection's active-set test (c > −10⁻³) and the reused factor with the 1.1 convergence-rate threshold.
  • Altro.jl's projection does not re-optimize. It takes the least-norm step onto the linearized active constraints, with a zero right-hand side for the gradient. pn_solve.jl's multiplier projection is disabled ("TODO: (#35) Re-implement this").
    • So full ALTRO is feasible to 1e-9 but 2.4e-5 and 1.4e-4 worse in objective than the augmented Lagrangian alone at 1e-6.
    • The states move by up to 0.05 on the cartpole.
    • A Scaly ALTRO that re-optimizes would need the cost gradient in the right-hand side. That is a one-line change, but it would no longer match Altro.jl.
  • The projection's footprint. The generated sparse L D L' of a ~1 100-row KKT matrix is 300 kB of C and 150 kB of workspace. That is fine on a PC and a real cost for an embedded target.
    • A Riccati-structured projection would be far smaller: the KKT matrix of an OCP is block-banded, and the stage-wise structure is known.
    • It would, however, stop sharing Altro's generic path.
  • One Scaly quirk found while building it. A SparseLDL's factor can be carried into a loop body and solved there with solve_with, but only through a Python-level handle on the SparseLDL object created while tracing the outer body. It works (the test pins it), but it is not an obvious API. A SymbolicLDL-level solve_with would make the pattern explicit. Not filed; noted here.

5. Tests and checks

  • New tests in tests/integration/test_case_study_altro.py, both self-contained:
    • test_three_nested_loops_reach_the_constrained_optimum: a compact augmented Lagrangian, around iLQR, around a line search, on a double integrator.
      • The problem has a bounded input, a goal constraint and a mask switching the first stage's bounds off.
      • The solution is checked against SciPy's SLSQP (to 2e-5; they agree to 1e-7).
      • It also checks that the bounds bind, that the masked stage exceeds them, that the three loop counts are consistent, that the outer index is right, and that a single-output scan works.
    • test_projection_reuses_one_factor_across_chord_steps: a pendulum's vmapped Euler defects, a KKT matrix from sparse_jacobian, and the factor passed into a while_loop and used with solve_with. It checks all four iterates' violations and the final point against the same chord iteration done densely in NumPy.
  • Mutation checks: 9 of 9 killed.
    • Loop nest:
      • unmasking the first stage;
      • reading the gains forward instead of backward;
      • reading multipliers and masks forward in the backward pass;
      • an off-by-one outer index;
      • a scan that drops its carry.
    • Projection:
      • the vmap reading the wrong next state;
      • a flipped right-hand side;
      • a shifted iterate index;
      • a dual regularization of 1e-2 in place of 1e-8.
  • Study gates, recorded in results/solve.json:
    • equal iteration counts on all four combinations;
    • trajectories within 8e-11;
    • the C-timed outputs equal to the Python call's.
  • Suite. The node-ID baseline is regenerated (+2). The case-study tests pass. The full suite was not rerun for this study: nothing under src/ changed, and the E3 timing grid was running on the same machine.

6. Fairness notes and what was not done

  • Machine. Not the reference machine. The load at process start was 4.3 to 5.9, under the gate of 6. The E3 grid was compiling QOCOGEN on one core during part of the run.
  • Timers differ. BenchmarkTools uses its own timer; Scaly's harness uses clock_gettime per call, which ticks in microseconds on macOS. That tick is below 1% of the smallest time here (115 µs).
  • Both sides compile natively. Julia uses LLVM for the host CPU; Scaly uses clang with -mcpu=native. Julia runs its default single thread (BLAS threads were not pinned, and nothing here is a BLAS-sized operation); Scaly's C is single-threaded.
  • Not done (CS-13).
    • Escape: Altro's infeasible start, with slack controls on the dynamics.
    • Quadrotor maze: a quaternion state, so iLQR in RobotDynamics' error state.
    • Kuka arm: RNEA as a scan over links.
    • DIRCOL: Scaly's IPOPT on a collocation transcription against Ipopt DIRCOL from Julia.
    The maze is the problem where the plan expected Scaly with IPOPT to beat Julia's DIRCOL.
  • No C++ ALTRO. C++ implementations exist (bjack205/altro-cpp, optimusride/altro-cpp, RoboticExplorationLab/altro) but were not built. Altro.jl is the reference the plan names.
