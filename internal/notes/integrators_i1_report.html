Integrators · I1 report

API-142 · explicit Runge-Kutta integrators, the start of scaly.integrators · branch claude/integrators-mpc · 27 Sep 2026 · plan integrators_mpc_plan_2026_09_27.md

Summary. scaly.integrators exists and discretizes a continuous-time model with any explicit Runge-Kutta method.

  • Maps with the model's signature. si.rk4(cartpole, dt=0.05) is a Function (x, u) -> xnext. The interval can instead be an input, and substeps are unrolled up to four and loop beyond. A template model gives a template.
  • Ten named methods plus user tableaus. Every tableau's declared order is checked against the order conditions, generated from rooted trees.
  • Same C as by hand. Coefficients fold the way a person writes them. A 40-stage shooting defect and its sparse Jacobian are byte-identical C to the hand-written RK4 of examples/nmpc_cartpole.py. The fix that got there removed a 6–8% gap.
  • Against CasADi. 13–23% faster than CasADi 3.8's fastest encoding on the step, the defects and their Jacobian. On the step's dense Jacobian it is at parity (+2%).

Suite: 2 459 passed, 42 skipped. The one failure is Colin's uncommitted test_tiny_qp_matches_the_closed_form: examples/tiny_qp.py calls the solver with one tuple, the convention from before templates P1b-ii. ruff is clean; ty is clean on everything this PR touches. 1. What was built

PieceDetails
integrators/tableau.py
  • Tableau(a, b, c, order, name, b_err) checks at construction:
    • the shapes;
    • that c holds the row sums of a;
    • the declared order, via order_conditions: b·Φ(t) = 1/γ(t) for every rooted tree up to that order. Trees are generated as canonical nested tuples; their counts match OEIS A000081.
  • TABLEAUS holds the named methods: Euler, Heun, midpoint, Ralston, Kutta's third order, SSPRK3, RK4 and the 3/8 rule. It also holds the Bogacki-Shampine 3(2) and Dormand-Prince 5(4) pairs, whose embedded weights are in b_err.
integrators/model.py
  • The model contract. The first parameter is the state, one float vector; the one output is its derivative.
  • discrete_map builds F over the model's own parameter list:
    • it keeps the names and a keyword signature, and adds a trailing dt when dt=None;
    • the output is {state}next;
    • above UNROLL_STEPS = 4 substeps the step runs in a scan, with a substep Function over the flat leaves broadcast at stride 0;
    • templates lift through Function._lift, as derivatives do.
integrators/explicit.py
  • explicit(f, method, dt=, steps=, name=) and rk4. Each stage is one call node of the model.
  • increment spells x + h Σ w_j k_j with a numerical h folded into the weights. Zero weights drop, and the most frequent weight (the smallest on a tie) is factored out. RK4 is therefore x + h/6 (k1 + 2k2 + 2k3 + k4), and Dormand-Prince's seventh stage (error only) is never built.

2. Tests and mutation checks

tests/integrators/ has 51 tests in three files, mirroring the package.

  • Order.
    • Every method converges at its order within 0.15 on a damped oscillator with input, against the exact solution by expm.
    • Five methods on Van der Pol, against solve_ivp(DOP853, rtol=1e-13), through the looped path.
    • Dormand-Prince reads about order 6 until rounding takes over (a tiny leading error constant), so there it is a lower bound. Its order 5 is pinned exactly on the linear problem.
  • Values.
    • A NumPy step from the tableau, to 1e-14. The model has a grouped slot with a matrix leaf and a scalar slot.
    • Looped substeps equal repeated single steps.
    • A dt input equals the folded interval, including under keyword calls.
  • Derivatives.
    • Jacobians in x and dt match central differences, unrolled and looped.
    • Reverse mode matches forward mode.
  • Structure.
    • RK4 makes 4 model calls and Dormand-Prince 6.
    • The C does not grow from 5 to 500 substeps.
    • A vmapped defect on si.rk4 generates the same C as a hand-written RK4. This is the benchmark gate, turned into a test.
  • Contract and refusals. A matrix state, a grouped state, a wrong output, a clash on the dt name, a nonpositive dt, zero steps, a plain callable, an implicit tableau, template naming.

3. Benchmarks

The benchmark is perf_2026_09_27_integrators/bench_explicit.py. The model is the cart-pole of examples/nmpc_cartpole.py, with two RK4 substeps of 0.025 s and N = 40.

  • Each cell is compiled with the JIT's flags and timed in C: 200 calls per sample for a step, 10 for a horizon.
  • Samples come from 7 interleaved rounds, and the fastest is kept.
  • CasADi is SX expanded or MX with a serial map, whichever is faster, and the table names it.

Apple M3 Max, Apple clang 21. These numbers are indicative, not reference-machine results.

  • First run. The library spelled RK4 as x + h(k1/6 + k2/3 + …) and x + h(0.5 k). That is two extra multiplies per stage and four at the end, which made it 5.8–7.6% slower on every piece, with otherwise identical C. Folding the coefficients closed the gap.
  • Now. Three of the four pieces are byte-identical C. The standalone step keeps the model out of line (83 lines against 180), which clang inlines again.
  • Driver. The IPM speed pass's driver timed one call per sample. macOS's clock ticks every 41.7 ns, so the step read as 0 or 41 ns. The copy here batches calls, and gives CasADi MX its integer workspace and pointer-scratch slots.
