Interpolation · review round (SPR) and summary

API-178 · branch claude/interp · 28 Sep 2026 · plan interp_plan_2026_09_28.md · tag interp-complete

Summary. Five reviewers read scaly.interp and the core changes it made, and reproduced every finding they reported. The dimensions were IR and AD, numerics, performance, API and docs, and tests with mutation coverage.

  • Bugs. They found about fifteen. Two of them returned another spline's code or tolerance: an explicit function(name), and the Function of an inverse(). Others:
    • a constrained fit went non-monotone at small data scales;
    • integrals were wrong past an end;
    • the basis strategy and reverse mode gave NaN far outside;
    • float32 tables lost accuracy far from the origin;
    • periodic fits of Expr data failed;
    • constant folding disagreed with C on NaN, leaving a cast of NaN (undefined behaviour) in generated code.
    All are fixed, with a test for each.
  • Tests. The tests reviewer's 26 new mutants had 24 survivors. The suite now kills 21 of the 26: three are equivalent, and two are defensive code that no reachable input exercises.
  • Two costly fixes rejected. Contracting coefficients before the local bases would have cost 1.7× in 2-D. Zeroing the outer cells' higher powers would have cost 8 to 12 % on every default evaluation. A hold of the continuation at 10^(300/k) replaces both and costs nothing.
  • Deferred. Eight items went to the todo list: C-187 (tables once per module) and API-188 to API-194. Three out-of-scope issues became separate tasks: the codebase map, the JIT cache key, and derivative caches that ignore nonsmooth.

Suite: 2 981 passed, 45 skipped; this round adds 80 test cases (38 new or re-parametrized tests). ruff and ty clean. Mutation checks: every review fix pinned (23 of 23 mutants undoing them killed, two after a test change, one more withdrawn as equivalent); the tests reviewer's 26 mutants: 21 killed, 3 equivalent, 2 defensive code.

1. What the reviewers found, and what changed

Finding (reviewer)Resolution
Constant folding evaluated minimum/maximum with NaN-propagating NumPy, while C's fmin/fmax drop NaN. A spline read at a constant NaN left (int64_t)NAN in C, which is out of bounds on x86. (AD)Fixed: the fold uses np.fmin/np.fmax. Test on the folded clamp and the generated C.
function(name) interned by name alone, so a second spline got the first's Function. Inverse.function() ignored tol/max_iter. (API, AD, tests)Fixed: interned by name and content, and an inverse's name carries its settings.
An Index from another partition was accepted, and read out of bounds. (API, AD)Fixed: an index carries its partition, and a spline on another refuses it; a derivative shares its spline's.
A periodic cubic or spline over Expr data failed, since the identity's columns do not close up. (API)Fixed: the identity reads the last value as the first.
smoothing dropped dtype. (API, numerics, AD)Fixed.
constrained was not scale-invariant: at data scales of 1e-10 its "monotone" fit had negative slopes. (numerics)Fixed: solved with the data and every constraint row scaled to one. The fix exposed a second problem: on a degenerate active set the polish's least-norm multipliers could take a wrong sign, so they now come from bounded least squares. The fit of s·y is now s times the fit of y to 1e-14.
A numeric integral wholly past one end was wrong. An Expr bound integrated the cubic continuation, not the tangent. (numerics, API)Fixed. Tails are exact for any interval, and in 1-D an Expr bound is exact in every mode, fill and periodic included. Its derivative in the bound is the spline there.
basis gave NaN at ±inf. Reverse mode gave NaN past about 1e150, from 0·∞. (numerics, AD, API)Fixed at no run-time cost, by holding the continuation at 10^(300/k) (10^(30/k) in float32), where the clamp for infinite points already was. That applies to linear continuations from degree 2 in both strategies, and to extend continuations under basis. Beyond the hold the value is frozen and the derivative zero; pp extend keeps its clean infinities. Documented. The two alternatives were measured first and rejected: contracting coefficients before the bases cost 1.7× in 2-D, and zeroing the outer cells' higher powers 8 to 12 % on every default evaluation.
float32 lost accuracy with the offset (1e-3 at 1e5), since tables were expanded about float64 centres. A float64 far-offset linear table erred 6e-7, from an 8-ulp tolerance on arithmetic centres. (numerics)Fixed: tables are expanded about exactly the centres the code holds. The float32 error at a 1e5 offset is now 6e-8; float64 arithmetic centres match to an ulp, and the table at 1e9 is exact.
inverse() stopped on absolute residuals, and its monotonicity check had an absolute floor. (numerics)Fixed: Newton stops on a step relative to the larger of |x| and the cell width, and the check is relative. Round trips reach rounding at y scales from 1e-10 to 1e6 and x scales down to 1e-16.
The periodic wrap was unbounded (5·10⁹ at x = 1e20). (numerics)Fixed: a point that has lost its phase is clamped into the interval.
constrained bounds hold only on the data's range, and periodic=True kept linear extrapolation. (API)Documented; periodic now defaults to periodic extrapolation.

2. Tests

  • The tests reviewer's 33 sketches are in the suite, adapted where the fixes changed the behaviour they pinned. Among them:
    • at() against evaluation at every edge, each side of it, and the midpoints;
    • 2-D Expr coefficients with a shared index;
    • NaN in any coordinate of a mixed-degree table;
    • the constrained fit's optimality (2-D least squares, 2-D pins, convex against trust-constr, the penalty order, extrap forwarded);
    • the polish's primal check;
    • the inverse's refusal of a cubic that turns inside a cell;
    • digests that tell apart splines differing only in fill or extrap.
  • A regression test for every fix above.
  • test_constrained.py's OCV "truth" fell near full charge. It now rises everywhere, an LFP-like curve, and the test first checks that plain least squares is not monotone.
  • The non-finite test runs under both strategies.

Two review-fix mutants survived their first run, until a test was tightened: the fold, where an outer fmax hid the inner minimum, and the monotonicity floor, which only matters below a 1e-14 scale. A third, the second pass of the periodic wrap, turned out to be equivalent. With fused multiply-add, one pass is exact where a point keeps its phase, and a point that has lost it is clamped either way. The second pass was removed. Of the tests reviewer's survivors, G4, G5 and K2 are equivalent, as the reviewer judged. C4 and C5 guard the polish against rounding-size singular values and weakly active constraints, which no test reaches: on dependent active rows the SVD gives exact zeros, and a weakly active constraint gives the same polished point either way.
