C-143 · a general dense solve: LU with partial pivoting · branch claude/integrators-mpc · 27 Sep 2026 · plan integrators_mpc_plan_2026_09_27.md

Summary. Scaly can now solve with any nonsingular dense matrix. The implicit integrators of I3 needed this: their Newton matrix I − h A⊗J is not symmetric.

  • linalg.lu(A) is a new expression op, P A = L U with LAPACK's pivot choice. Its factors and permutation equal SciPy's to 1e-12, including the pivot sequence.
  • linalg.solve(A, b, assume="gen") differentiates implicitly with two levels of rules, as SparseLDL.solve does. Jacobians match their closed forms to 1e-11, and Hessians and reverse-over-reverse derivatives work without the factorization ever being differentiated.
  • Against LAPACK dgesv (Accelerate), the generated solve takes 0.12× the time at order 4, 0.51× at order 8, and 0.94–1.05× from order 12 to 40. The kill criterion (above 3× at order 12 or less) is far away.

Suite: 2 488 passed, 42 skipped. The one failure is still Colin's uncommitted tiny_qp gallery test. ruff and ty are clean on src/ and the new tests. Mutation checks: 24 of 24 killed; three only after a test was added.

1. What was built

PieceDetails
ExprOp.LU (ir/expr.py)
  • The output is (n + 1, n). Rows 0..n-1 hold L (unit lower) below the diagonal and U on and above it. Row n holds the permutation as floats, so L U = A[perm].
  • The permutation is a row rather than a column so that the factors are a contiguous prefix. The triangular solves read F[:n] directly.
  • Carries the unroll attribute like the other dense factorizations. There is a verify rule (lu-shape) and a structural sparsity rule that is dense: pivoting moves any row anywhere.
Lowering (passes/lowering.py)
  • The algorithm. Right-looking elimination in place in the result. Per column:
    • the pivot search keeps a strict <, so the first row wins a tie, as LAPACK's idamax;
    • a full-row swap, the permutation included;
    • a divide down the column;
    • the trailing update.
  • Straight-line form (order ≤ dense_unroll). Every access is at a constant address. The swap is selects on the run-time pivot: n − k per entry of row k, and one per entry below it. That keeps the code open to scalar expansion.
  • Looped form. Swaps through loads and stores at pivot·n + j. The C does not grow with the order: 123 lines at 12, 24 and 40.
  • No new program op was needed: loads at computed addresses, select, compare, abs and casts were all there.
AD (ad/forward.py, ad/reverse.py)lu refuses a tangent or a cotangent in all three places a derivative can reach it (LU_NO_DERIVATIVE): single seed, multiple seeds and reverse. This follows the looped sparse factorization's precedent. A dependence check that finds no tangent never gets there.
linalg/dense.py
  • lu_solve(F, b, trans=False) is built from existing ops. It forms take of b at the permutation, then two solve_triangulars. With trans, it runs two transposed sweeps and then puts at the permutation.
  • solve(assume="gen") calls a Function (A, F, b) → x with F = lu(A) computed outside. It has a transposed twin. Both carry custom_derivative rules:
    • dx = A⁻¹(db − dA x);
    • in reverse, b̄ = A⁻ᵀ x̄ and Ā = −b̄ xᵀ, with the twin's formulas mirrored;
    • the factor's cotangent is a constant zero, so reverse mode never reaches lu.
  • Two levels of rules. Each level's rules call the other Function at the level below, so second derivatives are implicit too.
  • Matrix right-hand sides go through one vmap over the columns.
DocsA new section of docs/guide/linalg.md covers the op, the solves, derivatives, generated code and speed. The package map in docs/dev/codebase.md is updated.

2. Tests and mutation checks

tests/linalg/test_lu.py has 29 tests.

  • Factors. Orders 1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 13, 24 and 40 are checked on three kinds of matrix: one that needs pivoting (a zero corner and a large off-diagonal entry), a random one, and a permutation matrix. For each:
    • L U = A[perm];
    • the permutation equals SciPy's;
    • L and U equal SciPy's.
  • Ties. A tie picks the first row, and every multiplier is at most 1.
  • Solves.
    • Vector, matrix, transposed and transposed-matrix solves against NumPy.
    • Backward stability at cond 10¹⁰: relative residual below 1e-14.
    • A singular matrix gives non-finite values.
  • Derivatives.
    • The Jacobians in A and b and the adjoints are checked against their closed forms.
    • Reverse over reverse through the adjoint.
    • Hessians in A against finite differences of the gradient, in an objective that also solves with Aᵀ.
    • The factorization refuses in forward, multi-seed and reverse mode.
  • Code. Straight-line up to dense_unroll, loops above it with a size that does not grow. dense_unroll=0 forces the loops and gives identical factors.

What the three survivors needed:

  • L17. A reverse-over-reverse test. The transposed rule is only reached by differentiating the adjoint in reverse mode.
  • L21. A single-seed jvp. sc.jacobian takes the multi-seed path.
  • L24. A check that forced loops are loops in the C. The values agree either way.

Removed on the way: a sparsity= override on the general solve. It changed nothing, because the body's own pattern is dense and cheap, and an option with no effect is a defect by the codebase rules.
