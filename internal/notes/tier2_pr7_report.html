Tier 2 · PR 7 report

T2-7 · generated sparse LDLᵀ and solves (C-108) · branch claude/tier2-sparse · 26 Sep 2026

Summary. scaly.linalg.SparseLDL(K) factors a symmetric quasi-definite SparseMatrix without pivoting. Everything is generated code:

  • one scan per column segment from the T2-5 analysis, each step a left-looking column update;
  • all steps writing into a single carry vector with the run-time-index ops of T2-2;
  • every loop proven in place by T2-3, so nothing is copied between steps.

fact.solve(b) is a permutation, two sweeps and a diagonal scaling. It carries an implicit derivative, two levels deep, so gradients, Jacobians and Hessians never differentiate the factorization loops.

Gate: at most 2× a QDLDL-class C factorization. Kill criterion: more than 3× after one optimization round. After one round:

  • MPC and grid systems factor within 1.3–1.7×.
  • Random QP systems factor within 2.5×: over the gate, under the kill line.
  • Solves are within 1.3–1.6×, except a tiny grid solve at 3.0× (8 µs vs 3 µs).
  • Backward error is at rounding level (≤ 1e−13) at every tested δ.

The rest of the gap is the lane-table structure. C-109 (ragged loops, in T2-8) targets it; that change would also cut generation time, which grows with the flop count.

Suite: 1 344 passed, 88 skipped; the same four VM-only failures. ruff, ty and the docs build are clean. Mutation checks: 9 of 9 killed. One dead store was removed after its mutant survived: the work entry of the diagonal is never read again, so it no longer needs clearing.

1. The factorization

Carry layout. One vector holds [L below the diagonal (CSC of P K Pᵀ) | D | work w (n) | 0 | scratch (one slot per lane)].

Step j of a segment, as a chain of updates on the carry:

u1 = put(c, a_idx, take(kv, a_src))                                  # w[rows of K[:,j]] = K[:,j]
u2 = put_add(u1, u_w, -(take(c, u_lik) * take(c, u_dk) * take(c, u_ljk)))  # w[i] -= L[i,k] D[k] L[j,k]
wj = take(u2, w_off + j)
u3 = put(u2, d_off + j, wj)                                          # D[j] = w[j]
u4 = put(u3, c_pos, take(u2, c_w) * (1 / wj))                        # L[:,j] = w[rows] / D[j]
u5 = put(u4, clear, 0)                                               # clear the work rows used
  • Tables. Every index comes from the T2-5 tables, sliced one padded row per step. The in-place proof evaluates them for all steps and finds every read disjoint from the step's writes.
  • Padding in range. A padded lane reads the zero entry, or writes a scratch slot of its own after it. The ops then carry the new in_range=True promise, which drops their bounds checks.
  • Segments. The segment price includes the carry copy each loop makes on entry. MPC systems end up with 2 segments; random QPs with 12–19.

Optimizations made in this round

  • A take of one row now writes out[j], so producer fusion recognizes it. Its three gathers fuse into the put_add loop.
  • In-range padding removes every bounds check.
  • One reciprocal per column instead of a divide per entry.

The first version ran at 3.1–4.1× the baseline; after these changes it runs at 1.6–2.5×.

2. The solve and its derivative

  • Sweeps. The forward sweep scatters column j into the rows below it (put_add). The backward sweep accumulates a dot product into x[j], reading only rows below j. Both are in place, with in-range padding.
  • Derivative. solve is a custom_derivative Function of (factor, K values, b).
    • The factor's own derivative is declared zero, and everything flows through the values: dx = K⁻¹(db − dK x).
    • In reverse mode: b̄ = K⁻¹x̄, and K̄ = −b̄xᵀ on the entries the factorization reads, with a mirrored pair contributing through its lower entry and the upper copy getting zero.
    • The rules solve with a solve that has rules of its own, so Hessians are implicit too.

General changes this required

  • Constant-zero cotangents. custom_vjp_call hands back a rule's constant-zero cotangent as a constant, and reverse mode drops constant-zero contributions. Without this, the gradient differentiated the factorization loops through the factor's zero cotangent.
  • Multi-seed forward mode through a custom rule. This is now one vmap of the rule over the seeds instead of the per-seed fallback. Tangents the rule never reads (the factor's) are not formed.

3. Tests

tests/linalg/test_sparse_ldl.py has 44 tests; test_runtime_index.py gains one, for in_range.

  • Factorization and solves. Three matrix families × four orderings × three triangle inputs.
    • The factorization's componentwise backward error is below 1e−13·|L||D||Lᵀ|.
    • Vector and matrix solves match a dense solve.
  • Accuracy at δ ∈ {1e−4, 1e−8, 1e−10, 1e−13}. The backward error is < 1e−13, and the forward error is within 1000× of what SciPy's own solve reaches.
  • Generated code.
    • Every loop in the C is an in-place procedure.
    • Two solves share one factorization: exactly one call per segment.
    • The gradient's C holds only in-place loops, so the factorization is not differentiated.
  • Implicit derivatives.
    • The gradient in K's values matches finite differences.
    • The multi-seed Jacobian equals the gradient in strict mode.
    • The gradient and Hessian in b equal the closed forms 2K⁻ᵀK⁻¹b + … exactly.
    • The single-seed tangent matches the closed form.
    • With a full pattern, upper entries get zero gradient.
  • Validation. Non-square matrices, missing diagonals (now reported as such under auto, via TooMuchWork) and right-hand-side shapes are rejected.

Mutation checks (9 of 9 killed):

  • update sign
  • D offset in the update tables
  • clearing only scratch
  • the off-diagonal cotangent term
  • the off-diagonal term of K x
  • the zero-constant cotangent passthrough
  • the reverse-mode zero skip
  • multiply by D[j] instead of its reciprocal
  • missing diagonal scaling in the solve

Residuals are 2e−16 to 9e−16 relative, and D matches the baseline's to 1e−9.

  • Why random QPs lag. Their updates are many short column ranges. Each lane loads four index tables and three scattered values; the baseline walks one contiguous range per k.
  • Why generation is slow there. Generation time follows the update lanes (3.1 M → 10.8 s), not nnz(L). The <10 s gate is met at nnz(L) = 46 k for MPC (1.3 s) but not for flop-heavy random QPs.
  • The fix for both. Grouping the lanes by k into a run-time-length inner loop (C-109) keeps the tables at O(nnz(L)).
