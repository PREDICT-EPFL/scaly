Function templates P4: derivatives of templates

2026-09-27 · branch claude/function-templates · plan v2, step P4

Summary. Every derivative wrapper and custom_derivative accept a template. The result is a derived template: its instance for a call is the derivative of the source's instance, built on first use and cached. Names can be left out: one name is wrt (sc.gradient(f, "x"), as in the Expr form), and a name left out is the Function's only output or input (sc.gradient(energy)). Concrete sources give exactly what they gave before.

What changed

  • Lifting. Function._lift(transform, name, extra) makes a template whose instance for a call is transform of the source's instance for the call's leading arguments. extra names the arguments a seeded derivative appends (seed, lam); the source instance fixes their shapes, so they need no holes. The derived instance is cached per source instance. Its arity errors name the source's parameters plus seed/lam, through a synthesized positional-only signature.
  • The nine wrappers (jacobian, gradient, hessian, sparse_jacobian, sparse_hessian, forward, adjoint, lagrangian_hessian, sparse_lagrangian_hessian) go through one _derived helper.
    • A concrete source is derived at once.
    • For a template, the names it declares are checked at once. The checks include "'f' is an output of cost, not an input" with both spellings, and the defaults that follow from a single declared input or output.
    • Shape checks, such as a gradient needing a scalar output, happen per instance.
  • Names. A derived instance is named by the derivative from its source instance: cost__3_grad_f_x. With name=, the derived template takes that name and its instances are {name}__{tokens}, reusing the source instance's tokens, which derivatives of derivatives carry through (curvature__2).
  • custom_derivative lifts a template source. jvp/vjp rules may be templates: they are instantiated at the leaves they take, one per parameter, for the matching instance. For a concrete source this happens at once. Overloads keep a concrete source's result typed ConcreteFunction.
  • forward and adjoint take their names like the unseeded wrappers (*names, of=, wrt=). The Lagrangian Hessians' wrt is optional.

Tests and mutation checks

tests/function/test_lifted.py (8 tests) covers:

  • the gradient of a template compiled at two shapes, against the closed form, finite differences and the concrete source's own derivative;
  • nothing built until a call;
  • every spelling of the names, and the defaults on declared and bare templates;
  • eager name checks, and shape checks at the call;
  • forward/adjoint appending their seed, and the dense and sparse Lagrangian Hessians over a template;
  • name=, and derivatives of derivatives;
  • custom_derivative with template rules;
  • one derived instance for a numerical and a symbolic call at one shape.

The typing tests cover the one-name and no-name forms and the custom_derivative overloads.
