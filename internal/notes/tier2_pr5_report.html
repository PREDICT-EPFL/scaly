Tier 2 · PR 5 report

T2-5 · symbolic LDLᵀ analysis (C-105) · branch claude/tier2-sparse · 26 Sep 2026

Summary. scaly.linalg.analyze computes, in NumPy at generation time, everything the generated factorization in T2-7 will index:

  • a fill-reducing ordering;
  • the permuted lower triangle, with a map back to the input values;
  • the elimination tree and a postorder;
  • the row and column patterns of L;
  • the left-looking update lanes;
  • statistics;
  • a split into consecutive column segments, chosen by dynamic programming over a padded-work model.

Checks. The tables were checked by running the exact left-looking algorithm T2-7 will generate, in NumPy, on quasi-definite matrices. The factors reproduce the permuted matrix to 1e−12, whichever triangle of the matrix is given.

Numbers. The MMD fill equals SuperLU's on every test matrix. Analysis of a 100×100 grid (nnz(L) = 176 k) takes 60 ms, well within the <10 s at 50 k gate.

Suite: 1 197 passed, 88 skipped; the same four VM-only failures. ruff, ty and the docs build are clean. Mutation checks: 8 of 8 killed, after two tests were added.

1. What the analysis produces

PartHow
Ordering: natural, rcm, mmd, auto
  • RCM comes from SciPy's csgraph.
  • MMD is SuperLU's MMD_AT_PLUS_A column permutation, taken from an LU of a diagonally dominant matrix with the same pattern. perm_c maps old to new, so it is inverted; that inversion was found and fixed by the fill test.
  • auto analyzes all three and keeps the least update work. On stage-ordered MPC matrices this keeps the natural order: 4 600 vs MMD's 4 980 at N = 100.
Permuted lower triangle
  • Accepts one triangle, both or any mix; a mirrored pair is read from its lower entry.
  • a_source points each entry at the input values.
  • A missing diagonal entry is an error, because there is no pivoting.
Elimination tree, postorderLiu's algorithm with path compression; an iterative DFS for the postorder.
Pattern of LRow patterns by row-subtree traversal, linear in nnz(L); column patterns by sorting. Also r_pos, the position of each L[j,k].
Left-looking update lanes
  • For column j: one lane per L[i,k] with i ≥ j, for every k in row j, giving w[i] −= L[i,k]·D[k]·L[j,k].
  • Built vectorized from the row positions.
  • The analysis refuses before allocating when an ordering exceeds max_update_lanes (50 M).
Statisticsnnz(L), fill, flops, update lanes, tree height, fundamental supernodes, widest column, row and update.
Segments
  • Minimize Σ length × (step + a·max A + u·max U + c·max C) + price per segment.
  • Exact DP over chunk boundaries, with at most 2 048 chunks and vectorized running maxima. Brute force over every partition agrees at four segment prices.

2. Tests

tests/linalg/test_symbolic.py has 43 tests.

  • Pattern. Six matrix families (random, grid, arrow, tridiagonal, dense, block diagonal) × three orderings. The pattern of L equals a dense boolean elimination of the permuted pattern. The etree parent is the first subdiagonal row. The postorder puts children first. The row patterns and r_pos are consistent.
  • Tables. A NumPy run of the left-looking algorithm over the tables alone reproduces P K Pᵀ = L D Lᵀ. This covers four families and three triangle inputs, with every third diagonal negative (quasi-definite).
  • Triangles and orderings.
    • Lower, upper and full inputs give identical tables, and a mirrored pair reads the lower entry.
    • MMD removes the arrow's full fill (780 → 39).
    • RCM does not widen the band.
    • A given permutation is honored.
    • auto picks the least work.
  • Statistics. Path, dense and block matrices give the expected fill, height and supernode counts.
  • Segments. Segments are optimal against brute force and cover the columns contiguously. The chunked DP never does worse than a single segment.
  • Limits. The lane limit refuses bad orderings. Analysis at nnz(L) > 50 k finishes in under 10 s.

Mutation checks (8 of 8 killed):

  • MMD permutation not inverted
  • depth
  • supernode merge rule
  • DP without running maxima
  • mirrored-pair preference
  • update count off by one
  • unsorted rows
  • missing-diagonal check

  • Padding. Segments keep padding to about 1.1× on meshes with MMD. The MPC case at 1.8× in a single segment is a choice: its columns are so small that the per-segment price outweighs the lanes. A lower price would split it.
  • Nearly a path. The natural-order MPC tree is nearly a path (height 801 of 1 004), as the review predicted. That is what makes the stage-by-stage kernels of Tier 5 attractive.
  • SuperLU comparison. Fill matches SuperLU exactly on the small matrices. On the 100×100 grid it is within 1.4%: SuperLU applies its own postordering and relaxation.
