# Results, 2026-09-27

## I1: explicit RK4 (`bench_explicit.py --rounds 7`)

Cart-pole of `examples/nmpc_cartpole.py`, two RK4 substeps of 0.025 s per interval, N = 40. Fastest
sample over 7 interleaved rounds of 400 samples (200 calls each for a step, 10 for a horizon).

| piece | hand ns | lib ns | lib/hand | CasADi (encoding) ns | lib/CasADi | C lines hand / lib / CasADi | generate s hand / lib |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| step | 75.4 | 75.4 | 1.000 | 86.2 (casadi_sx) | 0.87 | 180 / 83 / 400 | 0.04 / 0.01 |
| step_jac | 204.0 | 203.5 | 0.998 | 198.8 (casadi_mx) | 1.02 | 420 / 420 / 1469 | 0.12 / 0.11 |
| horizon | 2570.8 | 2570.8 | 1.000 | 3037.5 (casadi_sx) | 0.85 | 98 / 98 / 11365 | 0.02 / 0.03 |
| horizon_jac | 6737.5 | 6791.6 | 1.008 | 8870.8 (casadi_sx) | 0.77 | 384 / 384 / 52566 | 0.15 / 0.13 |

`step_jac`, `horizon` and `horizon_jac` are byte-identical C for `hand` and `lib` (up to a hash in
one callee's name), so their ratios are noise. `step` differs in form: the library keeps the model
as one procedure called per stage (83 lines) where the hand-written step inlines it (180 lines);
clang inlines it again and the time is the same.

Before the coefficient folding in `integrators.explicit.increment`, the library spelled RK4 as
`x + h (k1/6 + k2/3 + k3/3 + k4/6)` and `x + h (0.5 k)`: 5.8 to 7.6% slower on every piece, the
same C otherwise.

## I2: dense LU solve (`bench_lu.py --rounds 5`)

A random matrix with a zero in its corner (so the first column must pivot) and one right-hand side.
Fastest sample over 5 interleaved rounds of 300 samples, each of `max(1, 20000 / n^2)` calls.

| n | form | scaly ns | LAPACK dgesv ns | scaly/LAPACK | C lines |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | unrolled | 14.4 | 118.0 | 0.12 | 182 |
| 8 | unrolled | 144.8 | 283.0 | 0.51 | 822 |
| 12 | loops | 500.0 | 518.4 | 0.96 | 123 |
| 24 | loops | 1674.0 | 1587.0 | 1.05 | 123 |
| 40 | loops | 5243.1 | 5569.4 | 0.94 | 123 |

LAPACK's call carries its own overhead (argument checks, the blocked driver choosing the unblocked
kernel), which dominates at small orders; from order 12 both are the same right-looking algorithm.
