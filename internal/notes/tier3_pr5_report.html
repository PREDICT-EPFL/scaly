Tier 3 · PR 5 report

T3-6 · the generated IPM's speed (C-132, with C-100) · branch claude/tier3-ipm · 26 Sep 2026

Summary. The target is met: with the sparse backend, the generated solver runs at 1.49× PIQP's warmed-up solve time, geometric mean over the 35 problems where PIQP takes at least 50 µs. Against PIQP's setup plus solve it runs at 0.91×.

  • Measurement first. T3-5's 1.96× was mostly measurement. PIQP's times came from one cold solve per process, ours were medians taken while Spotlight was indexing, and Apple Silicon runs the first calls of a burst up to 1.6× slower. Now both sides are warmed up and take the minimum over the same number of samples: the trace driver repeats PIQP's solve in-process.
  • Profile in C. Each piece of a step runs K times inside a generated loop. Per iteration, a sparse step costs 1.1–1.4× PIQP's; the factorization is 40–60% of it.
  • Changes:
    • The loop carries the residual vectors instead of recomputing them at the top of every step.
    • The Function-call layer passes conforming arrays through unchanged (C-100): a trivial call went from 4.6 to 2.9 µs, a 9-input call from 22 to 12 µs.
    • Each thread reuses one workspace.
    • The retry loop copies the factor once less (C-131).
    These changes account for 7–12% (A/B against the previous commit, same protocol).
  • Dense backend: 2.45× above the call floor. Its Cholesky kernel is 80% of a step and runs at a quarter of Eigen's speed, which is C-117's job.

Mutation checks: 8 of 9 killed; the survivor is equivalent. Suite: see the commit.

The protocol

Per problem, perf_2026_09_26_tier3/t3_4_ipm.py times the generated solve through the JIT: 0.2 s of warm-up calls, then the fastest of 50. PIQP's figure is the fastest of 51 solves in one process, from its own C timer (piqp_trace.run(..., repeat=50), SCALY_TRACE_REPEAT in the driver). For QSC205 the warm-up alone moved our time from 864 to 554 µs and PIQP's from 426 to 387 µs.

The generated time includes the Python call: about 3 µs plus 1 µs per input, so about 12 µs for a QP. On tiny problems that floor sets the ratio (HS21: 14 µs against a 9 µs C-timed solve; TAME: 14 against 1), so geometric means are reported both over all 51 problems and over those where PIQP's solve takes at least 50 µs.

Tests and mutation checks

  • tests/codegen/test_jit.py:
    • the workspace is reused, and a reused one full of NaN gives the same result;
    • calls from 8 threads overlap (ctypes releases the GIL) and each thread gets its own workspace. The test's scan step is now nonlinear, so that the gradient reads back the stored carries a shared workspace would corrupt;
    • a truly strided input (the old "strided" case, arange(6)[::2] / 2.0, was contiguous);
    • a wrong-shaped input through the flat call.
  • One fix to an order-dependent test from C-107: the compiler banner probe is now primed before subprocess.run is patched.
  • Call layer, 6 mutants:
    • killed: a workspace shared by all threads (only after the step was made nonlinear), both of run's checks (only after the strided and wrong-shape cases were added), and flattening's shape check;
    • survived: flattening's dtype check. It is equivalent, since run converts to float64 and the entry converts integers the same way.
  • Carried residuals: 3 mutants (not storing them after a step, or at the initial point, and reading two fields swapped); results below.

Carried residuals: all 3 mutants killed by the step-by-step and decision-trace tests.
