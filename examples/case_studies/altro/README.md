# Case study E4: ALTRO

ALTRO (Howell, Jackson and Manchester, "ALTRO: A Fast Solver for Constrained Trajectory Optimization",
IROS 2019), on its current implementation, Altro.jl 0.5, against the same algorithm generated by Scaly
as one C function. The problems are the paper's parallel park and cartpole in their 2019 definitions.

```bash
examples/case_studies/altro/baseline/setup.sh     # Julia 1.10.12 and the pinned Altro.jl environment, in baseline/third_party
uv run examples/case_studies/altro/compare.py --out examples/case_studies/altro/results/solve.json
uv run --with jupyterlab jupyter lab examples/case_studies/altro/altro.ipynb
```

`altro.ipynb` explains the study, builds and runs both of Scaly's solvers on both problems without
Julia, shows the generated C, and plots the recorded comparison. Its `RUN_BASELINES` flag reruns it.

## The algorithm

ALTRO has two phases.

1. **Augmented-Lagrangian iLQR.** iLQR on `J + sum (lambda'c + 1/2 c' I_mu c) + nu'g + mu/2 |g|^2`,
   with `g = x_N - x_f` the goal and `I_mu` the penalty on the active inequality rows. An outer loop
   updates `lambda <- max(0, lambda + mu c)`, `nu <- nu + mu g`, `mu <- 10 mu` until the violation is
   below a tolerance.
2. **Projected Newton.** States and controls become free variables. Each projection factors the KKT
   matrix of the cost Hessian and the active constraints (initial state, dynamics, inequalities within
   1e-3 of binding, goal) and takes the smallest step onto their linearization, reusing the factor
   while the violation falls superlinearly and backtracking on the violation.

Altro.jl runs phase 1 alone when `projected_newton=false` (it then stops at `constraint_tolerance`,
1e-6), and both phases otherwise (phase 1 to `projected_newton_tolerance`, set to 1e-4 here, then the
projection to 1e-6). Both configurations are compared.

`al_ilqr.py` writes both phases in Scaly, following Altro.jl 0.5's source rather than the paper's
pseudocode: the line search's acceptance ratio in `[1e-8, 10]` and its 20 halvings, the regularization
schedule (`1.6` growth, `1e-8` floor, `+10` after a failed line search), the inner stopping test (cost
decrease below 1e-4 and Altro's mean relative feedforward step below 1), the active set `c >= 0 or
lambda > 0`, the outer stopping test (violation alone), and in phase 2 the active-set tolerance, the
`1e-8` primal and dual regularization, at most three linearizations and ten line-search halvings, and
the `1.1` convergence-rate threshold. Every loop is a `while_loop` or a `scan`, the local models are
`sc.jacobian`, `sc.gradient` and `sc.hessian`, the KKT Jacobian is `sc.sparse_jacobian` of the
`vmap`ped stage constraints, and the KKT matrix is factored by the generated sparse `L D L'` over one
pattern (an inactive row is zeroed with a `-1` pivot), so the whole solve is one C function with no
allocation.

Where Altro.jl's projection recomputes each line-search trial's step from the rejected trial's
constraint values (`_qdldl_linesearch`, a line commented out), Scaly's reuses the step from the current
point. Neither problem rejects a full projection step, so the two agree here.

## The problems

The 2019 definitions from TrajectoryOptimization.jl at `320dbaca` (2019-07-18,
`problems/parallel_park.jl` and `problems/cartpole.jl`), ported to Altro.jl 0.5's API in
`baseline/run_altro.jl` and stated for Scaly in `scaly_impl.py`:

| Problem | Model | Knots, step | Constraints | Weights | Initial controls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Parallel park | Dubins car, `x = (p_x, p_y, theta)`, `u = (v, omega)` | 51, 0.06 s | `abs(u) <= 2` at every knot; `abs(p_x) <= 0.25`, `-0.001 <= p_y <= 1.001` from the second; goal `(0, 1, 0)` | `Q = R = 1e-2 dt I`, `Q_f = 100 I` | ones |
| Cartpole | `Cartpole(1.0, 0.2, 0.5, 9.81)`, `x = (s, theta, s_dot, theta_dot)` | 101, 0.05 s | `abs(u) <= 3`; goal `(0, pi, 0, 0)` | `Q = 1e-2 dt I`, `R = 1e-1 dt I`, `Q_f = 100 I` | 0.01 |

Both integrate with Kutta's third-order Runge-Kutta (RobotDynamics' `RK3`, Scaly's
`integrators.explicit(f, "rk3")`). The 2019 cost multiplied each stage term by `dt` and the current one
does not, so `run_altro.jl` scales `Q` and `R` by `dt`.

## Results

Apple M3 Max, macOS 26, Apple clang 21 at `-O2 -mcpu=native -fno-math-errno`, Julia 1.10.12. This is
not the reference machine of [fairness](../../../docs/results/fairness.md); none of this belongs on the
results pages. Every process started with the one-minute load between 4.3 and 5.9. Solve is the best
cold solve of the fastest of three fresh processes: BenchmarkTools' minimum over 50 samples for
Altro.jl, the minimum over 200 calls from C for Scaly (medians are within 3% of the minima on both
sides). The objective is recomputed from each side's own trajectory with one formula. Iterations are
Altro.jl's count, iLQR iterations plus projections.

First solve is the time from a fresh process to the first solution, less interpreter start-up: for
Altro.jl, package load (0.8 s), problem and solver setup (3.9 s) and a first solve that compiles (14 to
15 s); for Scaly, import (0.1 s), build, C generation and compile.

- **The same iterates.** Both algorithms on both problems take Altro.jl's iteration counts, reach its
  violation to every printed digit and its trajectory to 1e-10. Altro.jl's own `cost(solver)` differs
  from the objective in the eighth digit (0.03572541 against 0.03572539 for the parallel park's
  augmented Lagrangian); recomputed from the trajectories the two sides agree to 1e-17.
- **4.1 to 8.6x faster**, which meets the plan's criterion (the same optimum to 1e-6 relative, in less
  time than current Altro.jl) on both problems.
- **The cartpole gains less.** Its line search takes 3.5 rollouts per iLQR iteration against the
  parallel park's 1.4 (391 against 25, counted with a scratch variant of `al_ilqr.py`). Rollouts are the
  cheap part of an iteration, and the derivative expansions of the backward pass are the likely
  location of the gap. The split was not measured.
- **Altro.jl's projection finds feasibility, not optimality.** ALTRO's answer has the lower violation
  and the higher objective: 2.4e-5 relative above the augmented Lagrangian's on the parallel park,
  1.4e-4 on the cartpole, with states moving by up to 0.05. Altro.jl 0.5's projection takes the step of
  least norm onto the constraints; its multiplier projection, which would restore stationarity, is
  commented out (`pn_solve.jl`, "TODO: (#35) Re-implement this"). Scaly reproduces that, since the aim
  is the same algorithm.
- **The projection's price in Scaly is code and workspace**: 44 kB of C and no workspace for the
  cartpole's augmented Lagrangian, 348 kB and 18 788 doubles once the 1112-row KKT matrix and its
  generated sparse `L D L'` are added (211 kB and 8 189 doubles on the 809-row parallel park). Altro.jl's
  augmented Lagrangian allocates nothing; its projection allocates 204 and 404 times per solve.

The paper's Table I (i7-6500U, the 2019 implementation) reports 0.117 s for the parallel park, a
hundred times the current Altro.jl here: context only.
